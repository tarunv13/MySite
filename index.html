Where it started · Ranthambhore Tiger Reserve

Where the question started

2019225
202098

Recorded offences fell from 225 to 98 in a year.

My analysis was not that offending had stopped, but that cases were no longer being registered once COVID-19 arrived .

I had no second record to check it against, and that gap has shaped my work since. I now read every dataset for who made it, in which language, and who had no way to reach it.

01 · Research

Four blind spots I work on

  1. Who kept the record?

    A fall in reported crime can mean that fewer cases were recorded, so I read the record and the recorder together.

  2. Which language was it in?

    At WCS-India, a species that appeared absent in English was openly advertised in Hindi .

  3. Did people actually change?

    I use trials and interviews to check whether a programme changed what people do, and not only what they say.

  4. Who got to play?

    Badges, points and nature films ask people to care. I study what they change, and who gets left out.

05 · Quick answers

About Tarun Verma

Who is Tarun Verma?

Tarun Verma (Tarun Kumar Verma) is a conservation science researcher from India, raised in Udaipur, Rajasthan . He works on illegal wildlife trade, conservation behaviour change and impact evaluation, and builds open-source research tools such as WildTrace and Sift QDA. Before moving into science, he worked with Raghu Rai’s photography magazine from 2015 to 2018, where he learned to read an image for its framing, its timing and what it leaves out.

What has he worked on?

He led the India component of the impact evaluation of UNEP’s Tide Turners Plastic Challenge at ATREE, with the University of Oxford. At WCS-India he monitored online wildlife trade and built a lexicon of more than 50 trade codewords in Hindi and Telugu. At C-TARA, IIT Bombay, he coded three decades of Kalpavriksh’s Protected Area Update into a public, mapped database.

Which methods and tools does he use?

Randomised trials and pre-post surveys, interviews through translators, thematic analysis, QGIS, Python, text classification and open data pipelines. When a tool is expensive or missing, he builds a free one.

Is he open to PhD positions or collaboration?

Yes. He is looking for doctoral research in engaging media, conservation behaviour and digital evidence, and is glad to hear about collaborations. Message him on LinkedIn.
